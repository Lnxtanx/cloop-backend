The terms ‘government’ and ‘governance’ are closely related. However, they are not synonymous. Government refers to the institutions and authorities that exercise political power, while governance refers to the process by which decisions are made, implemented, and monitored in the public interest. The government formulates and implements people-centric policies, and good governance ensures that these policies are transparent, accountable, effective, and responsive to people’s needs. Governance is the system through which decisions are made, implemented, and monitored for the welfare of the people. It refers to the way a country, state, city, village, school, or organisation is managed. Therefore, understanding governance is essential to know how government impacts our lives.

Evolution of Governance in India

In the previous section, we studied that while the government is the institutional aspect, governance refers to its functional aspect. The government’s institutional setup influences the nature of governance, and governance initiatives, in turn, require institutional adaptations to implement policies and schemes.

We are familiar with the structure of government at different levels in India. But the same structure has not always existed as it does now; it has evolved from certain historical experiences. Let us understand this historical context by looking at the ‘big picture’ of the world history.

Do you remember the phenomenon of colonialism that you learnt about in Grade 8? We know that European powers colonised many societies, particularly those in Asia, Africa, and the Americas. Before colonial rulers forced their own models of governance and policy-making on colonised people, these societies governed themselves in accordance with local and organic systems. Although most colonies had become independent by the end of the 20th century, colonial institutions largely persisted, and their structures continued to dominate even in contemporary times. India is not an exception to the historical legacy of colonialism. However, India stands out in two important ways. Firstly, unlike many societies that experienced colonial rule, India has preserved most of its knowledge of its pre-colonial institutions and traditions of governance through texts and practices. Secondly, after Independence in 1947, it was not possible to replace all colonial institutions at once. The leaders of independent India therefore framed the Constitution in the spirit of India’s own values and ideals.

Good Governance

Governance affects many aspects of our daily life. It helps maintain law and order so that people can live safely and peacefully. It protects the rights and freedom of citizens, which are essential in a democratic society. It also helps people access important public services, such as education, healthcare, transport, and so on.

Good governance promotes justice and fairness and ensures that people are treated equally before the law. It also helps build trust between citizens and the government.

Governance is the government’s responsibility, but good governance depends on how different sections of society and government perform their responsibilities and work with one another. For example, the government makes laws and policies and provides public services. Citizens elect their representatives, follow the law, and participate in public life. The media helps people understand public issues and brings important matters to public attention. Civil society organisations work with communities and raise awareness about social concerns. The private sector contributes through economic activity and innovation.

Therefore, features of good governance include responsiveness, transparency, participation, the rule of law, accountability, effectiveness, and inclusiveness.

When people and institutions perform their responsibilities honestly and work together, governance becomes more effective and public welfare is achieved.

Challenges to Good Governance

The task of governance is always ongoing. With time, the needs, desires, and aspirations of people also evolve, creating new challenges to which the government and the institutions have to respond. For example, weak coordination between different institutions and departments can affect the implementation of government programmes. At the same time, unequal access to public services can leave some groups or communities behind, particularly those who face social, economic, geographical, or other barriers. Malpractices and misuse of public offices can prevent resources and benefits from reaching the people. Misinformation and fake news also pose a serious challenge to good governance. Addressing these challenges requires effective institutions, responsible officials, active citizens, and a continued commitment to the principles of good governance. So far, we have understood that governance is the process through which decisions affecting people are made, implemented, and monitored with governments, institutions, both public and private, and citizens playing an active role in the process. But governance ultimately needs concrete instruments to address public problems and meet people’s needs. Public policy provides that link; it translates governance into planned action by turning public concerns and government priorities into programmes, decisions, and solutions.

Public Policy

The Meaning of Public Policy

Every day, we experience the impact of public policies, often without even noticing it. The roads we travel on, the traffic signals that help us cross safely, the clean drinking water supplied to our homes, the electricity that lights our classrooms, the public parks where we play, and the waste collected from our neighbourhood are all examples of public services made possible through public policies. These policies help make our daily lives safer, healthier, and more convenient.

Suppose your school notices that many students bring single-use plastic bottles and wrappers in their lunchboxes and schoolbags

every day. As a result, a large amount of waste is generated, and the campus becomes dirty, leading to environmental degradation in the long run. To address this problem, the principal discusses the issue with teachers, students, and parents. Together, they decide to introduce a ‘Plastic- Free Campus Policy’. Under this policy, students are encouraged to carry reusable water bottles; plastic bags are discouraged; dustbins for waste segregation are placed around the campus; monthly awareness programmes are organised; and teachers are assigned with the responsibility of monitoring the campaign. These decisions form a policy because they provide a planned course of action to solve a problem and achieve a sustainable goal.

Similarly, governments make public policies to address problems that affect society as a whole. Public policy is a result of a course of action adopted by the government to solve public problems, meet people’s needs, and promote the welfare of society. Through this course of action, governments decide what should be done, why it should be done, who will do it, how it will be implemented, and how its success will be measured. Further, public policy is monitored and evaluated through a feedback loop, and necessary changes are made to the policy if required.

What is ‘Public’ about Public Policy?

The word ‘public’ refers to all the people living in a society or community. A policy becomes public policy when it is made for the public benefit, implemented by public institutions, and often financed with public money collected through taxes. Public policies address issues that concern society as a whole, such as education, healthcare, transportation, sanitation, environmental protection, and public safety.

Public policy is about responding to the needs and aspirations of the people. Therefore, people play an important role in shaping them. People identify issues, express their concerns, participate in consultations, provide suggestions, and share feedback after a policy is implemented. Governments use this feedback to improve policies over time.

Case Study—A Ramp to Better Healthcare

Smt. Shanti Devi, a 72-year-old woman, visits the hospital for a health check-up. On arriving the hospital, she finds that she has

to climb stairs to reach the Outpatient Department (OPD). There is no ramp or handrail to support her. At the same time, a man in a wheelchair arrives but is unable to enter the building because the entrance has only steps, with no wheelchair-accessible ramp. A visually impaired person may be able to climb the steps to enter the hospital but finds it difficult to locate different hospital departments because there are no tactile paths or Braille signs.

Many patients and their family members realise that the hospital infrastructure is not accessible to everyone. They decide to raise this issue with the hospital authorities and local representatives. A local disability rights organisation also highlights the problem through newspapers and social media. The district administration takes note of the issue and asks the Public Works Department (PWD) and the Health Department to inspect the hospital. After consulting doctors, hospital staff, architectural experts, organisations working with persons with disabilities (PwDs), senior citizens, and residents, a plan is prepared to make the hospital more accessible. The government approves the funds needed to construct ramps, lifts, handrails, tactile pathways, and accessible toilets, and to install clear signboards. Once the work is complete, the hospital becomes easier to use for older persons, persons with disabilities, pregnant women, patients, and children.

The Making of a Public Policy

A public policy is developed through a systematic process rather than a single decision. It usually begins when citizens, government officials, researchers, or civil society organisations realise that a new need has to be met, an existing problem has to be resolved, or a facility has to be made better in the interest of the people. Then the government studies the issue, collects information, and consults experts, stakeholders, and the public to understand different viewpoints and possible solutions. Based on these discussions, the government authorities prepare, review, and approve a draft policy. Once the policy is approved by the concerned authorities, it is translated into programmes and schemes, which provide a clear framework for different ministries, departments, and local governments to take specific action. The implementation of any policy is continuously monitored and evaluated to assess whether it is achieving its objectives. Feedback from citizens and other stakeholders is used to improve the policy over time. Thus, public policy is a continuous cycle of problem identification, consultation, policy formulation, implementation, monitoring, evaluation, and improvement. This process helps the government respond effectively to the changing needs of society.

private sector, and people, all have different but connected roles in good governance. People, therefore, have an important contribution to make in the process of governance. By voting, following laws, raising concerns, participating in public affairs, and giving feedback on government services, they can help improve the way public institutions function. In a democratic country like India, governance is guided by the constitution, which lays down the framework for governing the nation.

Good governance is essential to strengthening democracy, promoting sustainable development, and ensuring national progress. It depends on transparent institutions, accountable leadership, active citizen participation, and a strong commitment to the rule of law. Public policy is integral to good governance. Good governance and public policy are crucial foundations for achieving national development goals. Good governance is, therefore, an ongoing effort that will help realise the vision of making India a developed, prosperous, and self-reliant nation.

Before we move on…

Æ Governance refers to the process of making, implementing, and monitoring decisions for the welfare of people, while government refers to the institutions that exercise political authority to govern.

Æ Good governance is based on principles such as participation, transparency, accountability, the rule of law, responsiveness, and efficiency, ensuring that public institutions serve the people effectively.

Æ India’s understanding of governance has evolved from the ideas and values reflected in Sabhā and Samiti, Rām Rājya, and Rājadharma to the modern democratic system based on the Constitution and citizen participation.

Æ Public policy consists of decisions and actions taken by the government to solve public problems and promote the welfare of the people. Effective public policies are developed, implemented, monitored, and improved through a systematic policy process.

Æ Governance and public policy involve the combined efforts of many actors, including the government, institutions, civil society organisations, private sector, the media, and citizens, each performing complementary roles.

4 – Governance and Public Policy

Æ Policy coordination among different government departments, institutions, and stakeholders is essential for addressing public problems, responding to emergencies, and delivering public services efficiently.

Æ Active citizen participation through voting, providing feedback, obeying laws, community involvement, and holding public authorities accountable strengthens democracy and promotes good governance.
