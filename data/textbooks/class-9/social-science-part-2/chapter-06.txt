From Rooftop to Revolution

Chawl: A traditional, multi-storey residential tenement building, typically consisting of dense, single-room units.

In 1959, seven ordinary homemakers from a Mumbai chawl gathered on a rooftop with borrowed equipment, a recipe, and ₹80. Their goal was modest—earn some extra income by making and selling papads. They had no business degrees, no investors, and no master plan; instead, what they had was grit and unity. There were no factories at first; each woman took raw dough home every morning, rolled papads through the day between household tasks, and returned the finished pieces by evening with the condition that ‘the quality had to be consistent’. They decided to start a cooperative society where every member who joined would be an equal owner, and profits would be shared equally regardless of seniority.

In 1962, they registered the venture as Shri Mahila Griha Udyog Lijjat Papad, a cooperative society, where ‘lijjat’ means tasty in Gujarati. Afterwards, many branches opened across India and garnered thousands of members. In the 1980s and 90s, Lijjat expanded into other products like flour, masalas, and soaps, and exports began too, crossing a turnover of ₹100 crore. By 2023, the cooperative had over 45,000 women members, 83 branches, and an annual turnover of ₹1,600 crore.

The women begin work early in the morning, facilitated by pick-up and drop-off bus services. Lijjat also provides scholarships to

Let us assume that you and your friends decide to open a bakery together that offers snacks and desserts. How would you go about it? You have to consider many crucial questions, identify challenges, and plan at every stage before starting the bakery to turn this simple idea into a successful business venture. This is when a business plan is developed. It is a written document, a blueprint explaining the goals of the business and the steps to achieve them. Human Resources

People are the heart of every business. Human resources are the individuals employed by the entrepreneur who contribute to the business. For a bakery, functions, such as hiring bakers, helpers, cleaners, and a cashier; training staff in hygiene and baking standards, and ensuring a comfortable and motivating workplace fall under the human resources department. They helps build a strong team with required skills that fosters business growth.

Marketing and Sales

The marketing and sales team helps attract customers and convinces them to buy the products. In a bakery, this includes choosing a catchy name, promoting the business on social media, and offering discounts.

Some simple strategies include:

Using social media to share photos and videos for awareness among people.

Creating unique or seasonal items and customised desserts like mango-flavoured cake.

Offering discounts, deals, or special offers.

Promoting eco-friendly practices of recyclable packaging material.

Providing easy online ordering and delivery.

India’s Traditional Entrepreneurial Community

Entrepreneurship in India has deep roots among traditional communities, where skills, occupations, and occupational knowledge have been passed down through generations. Long before modern startups, these communities practised self-employment, innovation, and trade, forming the backbone of India’s local economy.

The Chettiar (Nattukottai Chettiar) community from Tamil Nadu is a well-known traditional entrepreneurial community. Historically, Chettiars were traders, bankers, and moneylenders, operating not only across India but also in Sri Lanka, Burma (now Myanmar), Malaysia, and other parts of Southeast Asia. They were known for:

Strong business ethics and trust-based lending

Excellent accounting and record-keeping skills

Reinvesting profits into trade, temples, education, and community welfare

The Chettiar community built wealth through trade in salt, rice, textiles, and finance, often without formal banks. Even today, Chettiar entrepreneurship inspires modern India by showing how risk-taking, discipline, and long-term vision can build sustainable businesses rooted in tradition.
