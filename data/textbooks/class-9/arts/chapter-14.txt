Note to the Teacher

The Visual Arts chapters lay emphasis on daily practice, guiding students in the application of fundamentals as they discover the elements and principles of art and design in every aspect of their everyday life and surroundings. They will also expand their knowledge of materials, mediums and techniques, and learn to create their own compositions in a systematic and iterative way. You may modify the activities to suit your local context and the specific needs of your students. To ensure the best learning experience for every student, here are some points that need to be kept in mind.

The Visual Arts classroom needs:

adequate space for students to sit and work comfortably;

sufficient lighting and ventilation;

access to and proper storage of art materials, tools, basic stationery, and audio-visual facilities; and

space for showcasing and exhibiting artwork periodically.

The Visual Arts pedagogy would need to:

use storytelling and everyday examples when introducing concepts and processes;

encourage students to freely express their original ideas, imagination, emotions, and curiosities in their artwork;

encourage keen observations of everyday life and discourage copying images from other pictures and photographs;

allow students to explore, investigate, experiment, collaborate, and have discussions (or engage in dialogue) in the classroom;

include outdoor activities periodically, for example, drawing plants, animals, people, and objects from their surroundings in different seasons;

organise field trips; visits to museums, heritage monuments, and melas to enable students to observe the life and culture around them;

invite local artists and craftspeople to conduct interactive sessions and workshops that offer students hands-on experiences;

emphasise routines of cleaning up after work (such as putting back materials they use and maintaining art materials with care); and

Exploring the Fundamentals of Visual Arts

Art is a reflection of society. It shows how people have experienced life at different stages through the ages, and how they have responded to their surroundings. The Visual Arts is a very powerful means of expression, especially when you cannot find words to express something.

Making visual artwork will help you discover your hidden strengths and unique qualities. You will be able to spend long periods of time imagining, reflecting, pondering, researching, and experimenting in creative ways.

Through the Visual Arts, you will explore unknown terrains. Since explorations need preparation, read the following section carefully and discuss every point in class so that you can help one another while facing challenges during your discoveries.

Art Journal

A journal is a documentation or record of everyday experiences seen in different forms, such as handwritten diaries, sketchbooks, blogs, vlogs, photographs, reels, etc. Journals can contain text, drawings, pictures, moving images, sounds, and tactile materials.

Journalling is a healthy practice that helps us reflect on the way we think, emote, and relate to the world. For a visual artist or student, a journal is an important personal space for practice and growth.

You will use your journal through all activities. At the end of the term, you can compile your journals, sketches, drawings, and completed artwork to present them as your Visual Art portfolio.

Different Ways of Journalling

Drawing or sketching: It is used by artists, architects, and designers to visualise their ideas; make small studies; and work out solutions to problems.

Collage: It is used by artists who are interested in printed matter or tactile surfaces. They collect pictures from newspapers or magazines, textile scraps, samples of woven fibres, etc., and paste these in their journal.

Photography: Smartphone cameras have made photography accessible to many people. People create digital photo journals based on different themes that interest them.

Videography: Short videos lasting a few seconds to a minute are also a form of journalling. This is also a common digital medium these days.

Important reminders when journalling

1. Focus on being authentic when you record your observations, thoughts, and experiences. Avoid attempting to impress others.

Fig. 14.2. Journalling a scene at home

2. Write the date, time, and place in each entry. For example, you could draw a quick sketch of an object or a person you have observed at home. Next to the drawing, write the date, place, and time like this: DD/MM/YYYY, 00:00 a.m./ p.m., sitting at home with my family.

3. Include notes and thoughts with your drawings and pictures in the journal. For example, if you have pasted an interesting clothing pattern cut-out from a magazine, write about the elements that interest you and why.

4. Set weekly goals and track your progress. You could decide to make one entry a day or spend more time on alternate days. At the end of each week, spend 10 minutes looking at your journal, check that you have written — the date, time, and other details — and assess your progress.

5. Consolidate your journals at the end of each term. You may have made entries in more than one book.

Sketching

It is an exercise for spontaneous observation and drawing, which is used across many disciplines. It is similar to doing a warm-up and stretches practised by sportspersons. It is therefore very important to practise sketching regularly while observing your surroundings.

Fig. 14.4. A special child sketching. Artists cross

Avoid copying from other pictures. Remember, the first steps are always messy. When you start sketching initially, it is natural to experience dissatisfaction and disappointment in your work. This is when your dispositions help you to pick up courage, commitment, and perseverance. When you observe more carefully and sketch repeatedly, you will see your sketches improve in a few weeks.

Sketching tips

1. Sketches need not be perfect or contain too many details.

2. Position yourself at a reasonable distance from the object or living being that you draw so that you can see the forms clearly.

3. Always visualise the basic shapes before you draw.

4. Draw the shapes lightly and quickly.

5. Draw multiple times on the same image without using an eraser so that you can see your mistakes, corrections, and improvements.

6. Observe the negative space and ensure that your sketch fits within your page.

7. Capture the basic structure, placement, proportions, and postures along with the actions.

8. Darken the lines and add details only when you are satisfied with the basic form and structure.

9. Focus on the important parts while adding details.

1. Revise the principles of visual design

The principles of visual design are important concepts that help us improve the aesthetic qualities of artworks. These principles guide the structure, arrangement, and overall composition of visual elements.

The six principles you will examine are:

unity and harmony;

balance;

rhythm and movement;

emphasis;

proportion; and

hierarchy (order of importance).
