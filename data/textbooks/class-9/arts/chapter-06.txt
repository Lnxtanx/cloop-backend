Note to the Teacher

You have been our associate since the Preparatory and Middle stages for all transactions in the classroom. Let us move ahead to Grade 9 for further experiences in Music. Over the course of this year, this textbook will be a companion for you and all your students to go deeper in your exploration of music. By now, your students have experienced the wide array of expressions in music which cover multiple dimensions. Chanting, patriotism, devotion, storytelling, people's lives and occupations, environment, etc., can all be expressed through varied styles of rendition. Moving further, let us find out about different communities, regions, and people who have contributed to this vast ocean of genres/ vidhā.

In the previous class, the science of music was discussed. Did your students understand the intimate relationship that music has with science? We would like to take this a step further as well. This year let us apprise the learners about the anatomy of voice production and the science behind the production of sound from different musical instruments. Do experiment these theories in your class and help your students learn by practising.

Rāgas and rhythmic cycles can help them understand concepts in mathematics. Find out more about such multidisciplinary aspects of music. Collaborate with different teachers in your institution.

The classical music section will enable the learning of rāgas. Let the learners explore the concept of the number of notes that vary in these rāgas creating unique tunes due to the patterns known as jātis.

Musicologists such as V.N. Bhatkhande and Venkatmakhi have given classical music a systematic, scientific, developed understanding. Do let the learners delve deeper to understand the contribution of these stalwarts.

Also, we are certain they will be excited about Global Music. Make them understand how various popular styles prevalent in India have impacted International Music. We have given them some examples. Let them find out what are the styles incorporated in a fusion. We wish you sing and enjoy with your students and make this world a better place for all.

Also, it would be of great value to you if all their experiences in singing and research can be documented in a journal.

Can you imagine a world without music, where a mother does not sing lullabies, a wedding does not have songs, and no one hums a tune while going about their daily lives? Seems quite dull, doesn’t it?

Music is an important part of our lives in many ways. We all have songs and styles of music that we love and identify with. Music can tell us a lot about who we are, and where we come from. It connects us to our deepest emotions. Let us take a closer look at how music can be explored, so that we understand ourselves, our traditions, and our country better.

Forms and Styles of Music

Music reflects variations in geography, language, and culture. As Indians, we can take great pride in having one of the most diverse musical cultures in the world. In order to make sense of the diversity of music, we can classify it in many ways.

What is a genre?

A genre (also referred to as form or vidhā) is a category of music that has characteristic features and sounds. Śhailī refers to a style of presentation within that category. A genre is defined by the instruments it uses, the style of singing, its rhythms, and even where it is from. Identifying genres helps us classify music and understand it better.

Learning by listening

By the time he was 12 years old, the renowned musician Kumar Gandharva was known to imitate and exactly reproduce records of the legends like Abdul Karim Khan and Fayyaz Khan. At the age of 23, he fell seriously ill with tuberculosis and lost his voice.

Fig. 6.2. Kumar Gandharva

Confined to bed for years, he listened to folk songs of the village drifting through the windows, to temple bells, birds, and wind. When he finally sang again, his voice had changed, but his music was deeper, fearless, and unforgettable. By listening, he discovered a whole new world of music.

Let us recall

You were introduced to active listening in Grade 7, where you learnt to carefully listen to and analyse a piece of music. The more you listen to a wide range of music, the more you start to pick up on different styles, structures, and techniques that you might not have noticed at first.

Discussion circle

When you listen to a piece of music, you may notice that it has an underlying structure. For example, a devotional song will have a chorus (pallavī or sthāyī) followed by two or more verses (charaṇas or antarās). A contemporary song sometimes starts with an introduction before the chorus. Discuss the structure of the song that you just listened to, and any of its unique features.

While there are many types of music in India, most music can broadly be categorised into three areas, as below:

Type of Music Characteristics

Classical/ Semi–Classical Music

Ancient and living traditions, rāga-based melodic system, variety of tālas (rhythm cycles), emphasis on improvisation

Folk/ Indigenous/ Regional

Community-based musical expressions, rooted in regional life, language, and customs; transmitted orally across generations; closely linked to everyday activities, rituals, festivals

Popular/ Film Music

Hybrid in nature and borrows from a variety of Indian and global influences, reflects contemporary themes and trends

Music and Emotion

When you listen to various kinds of music, some forms of music may appeal more than others to you. Have you wondered why? It may partly be due to the style of music being something you are familiar with, or in a language that you can understand. Maybe the music has a rhythm that energises you or a melody that soothes you.

Music and patriotism

Songs can invoke a deep sense of love for the country. During India’s freedom struggle, songs like Vande Mātaram ignited the spirit of patriotism in the hearts of millions of Indians. In 2025, we celebrated the 150th anniversary of this evergreen song. Listen to it and sing it in class while focusing on the emotions and the imagery it creates.

Music, mood, and navarasa

Fig. 6.4. Bankim Chandra

Chatterjee

Indian treatises describe the navarasa [nine emotions: Śhṛiṅgāra (love), vīra (courage), karuṇa (compassion), hāsya (joy), raudra (anger), bhayānaka (fear), bībhatsa (disgust), adbhuta (wonder), and Śhānta (peace)]. Music has the power to awaken these rasas within us. A calm piece of music can lead us to Śhānta rasa, while a powerful rhythm can inspire vīra rasa. By choosing what we listen to, we gently guide our emotions.

Do you know?

Music therapy is the use of music — such as rhythms, melodies, and sounds — to improve mental health and overall well-being. It plays an important role in reducing stress, anxiety, and physical tension.

Creating my playlist

A playlist is a group of songs that are put together for a reason, either by a listener, an artist, or a music platform. Playlists can be based on the theme of the song, like ‘songs about rain’, the type of music, like ‘Indipop playlist’, or even the reason they are listened to, like ‘music while studying’. A playlist is not just a collection of songs, but can also be a tool to mirror and balance our emotions.

Activity 6.7

My family tradition

Families often have traditions relating to festivals, rituals, recipes, etc., that are shared across generations.
