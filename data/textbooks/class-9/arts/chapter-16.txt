Composition is a term used across art forms. A good visual composition is created when the elements of art are arranged by applying the principles of visual design.

Compositions can be either two-dimensional, like paintings and textiles, or three-dimensional, like free-standing sculptures, installations, and reliefs.

The terms ‘realistic’ or ‘naturalistic’ are used for artworks where the images resemble objects, nature, and people as they appear in reality.

The term ‘abstract’ is used to describe artworks which are imaginative creations, which may not have any direct resemblance to objects seen in reality.

Some compositions are a combination of both naturalistic and abstract imagery.

Compositional layout is the arrangement of all the visual components of an artwork, where the size, position, alignment, colour, and proportion of each component correlate with the rest to form a harmonious picture. For example, a product advertisement in a newspaper has text of varying sizes and colours which are placed along with images to convey a specific message.

A compositional layout can have multiple possibilities and arrangements, which influence the visual impact, meanings, and emotions it conveys. Therefore, it is important to experiment with different images and arrangements in the layout before finalising one. Each version that is created in the process is called an iteration. For example, you could create three layouts where you change the size or placement of a person/ object in each iteration.
