ACTIVITY 5.1: RANGE

INTRODUCTION

Welcome back, students! When we listen to a piece of music or sing a song, we observe that it is made of constituent elements.

Earlier we learnt about the concept of vocal range, that indicates the span from the lowest to the highest note you can comfortably sing. Extending your range is a great way to develop your singing.

Some of the main elements of music are — melody (raga), rhythm (tala) and lyrics (sahitya). In this chapter, we will discover further aspects of rhythm, melody and lyrics, and observe how they come together when we sing a song.

These vocal exercises help you practise the lower register (mandra saptak) of your voice. When practising, remember to keep your head and neck in a neutral position to avoid straining your voice or throat muscles.

ACTIVITY 5.4: SANSKRIT VERSES AND RHYTHM

Meaning

(Salutations to him) Who resides in the spiritual heart centre as omkara, on whom the yogis constantly meditate.

Who grants all desires and also liberation to his devotees. Salutations to that Shiva, who is represented by syllable ‘Om’.

There are many verses in Sanskrit composed by various poets and philosophers. These verses are set in a particular metre or pattern. This is referred to as chhandas in Sanskrit. Thus, while Sanskrit verses are chanted as a devotional offering, they can also give us an understanding of rhythm.

When you recite these verses a few times, you may observe that the syllables naturally follow a pattern of 4 which is also anushtup chhand.

Shloka and Chhand (Metre)

You can even clap your hands while reciting, to understand the underlying rhythm pattern.

Here is a shloka which you can recite in ʻAnushtup Chhandʼ.

ॐकारं बिंदुसंयुक्ं नित्ं ध्यायंति योगिनः ।

कामदं मोक्षदं चैव ॐकाराय नमो नमः ॥१॥
