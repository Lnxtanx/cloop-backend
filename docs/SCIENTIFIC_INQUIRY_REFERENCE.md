# Scientific Inquiry: academic reference for the replay

Use this to inspect the topic's supplied notes and the resulting chat. Match vocabulary to the selected board, textbook edition and grade. This reference states the underlying science; it does not certify complete board-syllabus coverage.

| Concept | Accurate teaching |
|---|---|
| Scientific inquiry | Investigating questions about the natural world using observations, evidence and reasoning. |
| Curiosity and questioning | Curiosity motivates questions. Observations can spark questions, and questions guide further observation and investigation. This is not one compulsory fixed order. |
| Observation | Information obtained directly through appropriate senses or measuring instruments. An investigation does not require all five senses. |
| Inference | An explanation or conclusion based on observations and prior knowledge. It may need further testing; it is not simply an unsupported guess. |
| Evidence and uncertainty | Distinguish what was actually observed, a supported interpretation, and a possibility for which no evidence has been supplied. |

## Correct the transcript's examples

- **“Ice feels cold.”** This is an observation from touch. It does not establish that the temperature is below zero. Ice can be at 0°C under ordinary conditions; use a suitable thermometer to establish temperature.
- **“The ball is on the ground, so it is stationary.”** Position alone does not establish rest. A rolling ball is also on the ground. Observing that its position remains unchanged over time can establish rest relative to those surroundings.
- **“The cricket ball is red.”** Colour must be established by the scenario or observed, not inferred from the object's name. Cricket balls can have different colours. Seeing a yellow ball directly is an observation.
- **“It may be yellow.”** Without an actual observation or supporting information, this is a possibility. Do not automatically call every guess an evidence-based inference.
- **“The road is wet; it may have rained.”** The wet road is an observation. Recent rain is a possible inference; sprinklers or another water source could also explain it.
- **“Eyes.”** Sight is one appropriate means of observation. Acknowledge it, then teach other appropriate methods if required. Do not demand all five senses without an explicit task; do not suggest tasting unknown substances or smelling them directly.
- **The tyre in sunlight.** Do not casually present a tyre becoming softer in the sun as an established typical effect. Warming air in an intact tyre commonly increases pressure. Check the observation and conditions rather than inventing a premise.
- **The student's boiling-water question.** Explore the actual observation: how long do bubbles continue after removing the pan from the flame? Stored heat in the pan can briefly continue heating the water. Distinguish this from prolonged boiling or bubbles caused by other processes.

## Questions and fair marking

“Name all 5 components of scientific inquiry” explicitly requests the five steps of the scientific method in NCERT Curiosity Grade 6, Chapter 1: observing, asking a question, guessing a possible answer, testing the guess (through experiments or more observations) and analysing the results. “Which sense did you use?” can be answered with one appropriate sense. For observation/inference recall, ask explicitly for definitions and examples only when those are the criteria being assessed.

For an incomplete answer, acknowledge the correct part and identify the precise missing point. For an unsupported temperature or motion conclusion, explain why the stated observation cannot establish it. Do not reinforce it as correct merely because it is phrased as an inference.
